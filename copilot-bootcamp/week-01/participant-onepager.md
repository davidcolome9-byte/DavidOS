# Week 1: Copilot as Researcher

**One hour. Print this or keep it open in a second window.**

## The idea in one sentence

Copilot is fast at finding and summarizing, and it is only trustworthy when you can see where the answer came from.

## What you learn today

- The difference between Copilot answering from your work content and answering from general knowledge
- Why Copilot cannot see anything you do not already have permission to see
- How to read and click through the source references in a response
- How to ask for a specific shape of answer instead of a vague one
- How to make Copilot tell you what is missing, which is usually more useful than what is present

## What you practice today

You get a synthetic policy pack for a made up credit union. Nothing in it is real. You will:

1. Ask a broad question and notice that the answer is not usable on a live call
2. Ask again with a defined format and scope
3. Ask Copilot to identify gaps in the source material
4. Check every factual claim in the output against the source text
5. Write a short briefing an agent could actually use while a member is on the line

## The pattern to remember

**Ask. Shape. Verify.**

Ask gets you something. Shape makes it usable. Verify makes it safe to act on.

## Prompt scaffold to start from

Use this until you do not need it anymore.

```
Using only the text I pasted above, answer this question: [question]

Format: [bullets, table, short paragraph, numbered steps]
Length: [under X words, or X bullets]
Scope: [what to include and what to leave out]
If something is not stated in the text, say "not stated" instead of guessing.
```

That last line is the one people skip and the one that matters most.

## What this costs you outside the session

About 15 minutes. If you do not have that off the phone, do the first two items and skip the third. Two thirds submitted beats nothing submitted, and that is an operational statement, not encouragement.

## What you submit

Post one reply in the Week 1 thread by end of day Friday containing:

1. Your final prompt, copied in full
2. Your final briefing output, copied in full
3. Two sentences on what you changed between your first attempt and your last

That is it. No formatting required. No polish required. If your result was bad, submit the bad result and the two sentences. A documented failure counts as a submission and is genuinely more useful to the group than a clean success.

## What good looks like

A strong Week 1 submission has a prompt that names the format, names the scope, and tells Copilot what to do when information is missing. The output reads like something you could glance at during a call, not like an essay. The two sentences describe a specific change, for example "I added a line telling it to say not stated, and it stopped inventing a 45 day timeline that was never in the document."

## What weak looks like

A single prompt like "summarize this," an output that is three dense paragraphs, and a reflection that says "it worked well." There is nothing in that for anyone to respond to, so nothing comes back.

## Ground rules for the whole six weeks

- Synthetic data only in every exercise. No real member names, account numbers, card numbers, case IDs, or screenshots of live systems.
- If you are not sure whether something counts as real, do not paste it. Ask first.
- Anything you plan to act on gets its source clicked and read.


## If you get stuck

Put it in the meeting chat. Faster than working around it.
