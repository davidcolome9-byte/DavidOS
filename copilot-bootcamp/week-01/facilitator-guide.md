# Week 1 Facilitator Guide: Copilot as Researcher

**Session length:** 60 minutes
**Format:** Teams meeting with cameras optional, chat used for submissions
**Cohort size assumption:** 12 to 16 participants [ASSUMPTION]
**Prep time for you:** 20 minutes

## What you are trying to accomplish

By the end of this hour, every person in the room should have used Copilot to answer a work question they could not answer from memory, and should be able to say out loud where the answer came from. That second part is the whole point. Week 1 is not about speed. It is about teaching people to treat Copilot output as a draft with a citation trail, not as a verdict.

If people leave believing "Copilot knows things," you have made your job harder for the next five weeks. If they leave believing "Copilot finds things and I check them," everything else lands.

## Before you start

1. Open the Week 1 exercise file and copy the synthetic source pack into a Teams chat message or a OneNote page participants can reach. They will paste it into Copilot chat.
2. Have your own Copilot window open and already signed in. Do not troubleshoot your own login on the call.
3. Post the announcement 48 hours ahead and again 2 hours ahead.
4. Decide where submissions go. Default is a reply in the Teams channel thread. [ASSUMPTION]
5. Have a fallback demo ready in case Copilot is slow or returns something odd. A screenshot of a good result you captured earlier is enough.

## Run of show

### 0:00 to 0:05 Open and frame

Do not do introductions. People know each other. Open with the problem instead.

Say something close to this:

> "Everybody in this room has had a moment where a member is on the line and you know the answer exists in a document somewhere, but you cannot find it in the time you have. Six weeks from now every one of you is going to have a faster way to handle that moment. Today we start with the least glamorous piece, which is finding things and knowing where they came from."

Set the ground rule immediately: no real member data, no real account numbers, no screenshots of live systems, in this session or any other session in this series. Everything we use is made up. Say it once, clearly, and then move on. Repeating it every five minutes makes people nervous rather than careful.

### 0:05 to 0:15 Teach: what "research mode" actually means

Cover four things and nothing else.

**One. Copilot has two very different jobs.** It can answer from your work content, meaning your Teams messages, emails, files, and meetings that you already have permission to see. It can also answer from general knowledge or the web. These produce different quality and different risk. Teach people to notice which one they just got.

**Two. Copilot cannot see what you cannot see.** Permissions carry over. If you do not have access to a SharePoint site, Copilot will not read it for you. This calms a lot of nerves in a regulated shop. Say it plainly.

**Three. The citation is the deliverable.** An answer without a source is a rumor. Show participants where the reference links appear in the response and how to click through. Anything they plan to act on gets clicked.

**Four. Ask for the shape you want.** "Summarize this" is weak. "Give me five bullets, each under 15 words, covering only what changed" is strong. This is the seed of Week 3, so plant it here and do not over explain it yet.

Anticipated question at this point: "Is it reading my email right now?" Answer: it looks at your content when you ask it something, in the moment, under your own permissions. It is not building a private file on you. Do not oversell. If you do not know the exact tenant configuration, say you will confirm and follow up, then actually follow up.

### 0:15 to 0:25 Live demo

Share your screen. Use the synthetic source pack from the exercise file.

Demo in three passes so people see the improvement, not just the ending.

Pass one, deliberately weak. Paste the source pack and ask: "What does this say about provisional credit?" Read the answer out loud. It will be broadly correct and mostly useless for a live call.

Pass two, add shape. Ask again: "From the text above only, list the provisional credit timelines. Use a bullet per scenario. Include the trigger event, the deadline, and the exception. Do not add anything that is not in the text."

Pass three, stress test it. Ask: "Which of those timelines is not stated in the text? Tell me what is missing that a contact center agent would need." This is the move that sells the whole session. Copilot will name gaps, and participants will see that the tool is more useful when you ask it to find holes than when you ask it to be confident.

Narrate what you are doing as you do it. Do not narrate what Copilot is doing. People can read.

### 0:25 to 0:45 Hands on

Send people to the exercise. Twenty minutes, working alone.

Stay on the call with your mic open. Do not fill the silence with talking. Watch chat. The two most common stalls in Week 1 are people who cannot find the Copilot entry point and people who write one prompt, get a mediocre answer, and stop.

For the first group, drop the direct link in chat once at 0:26 and once at 0:33.

For the second group, drop this line in chat at around 0:35: "If your first answer was fine but not great, do not rewrite the whole prompt. Just add one sentence telling it what format you want."

At 0:42, give a two minute warning and tell people to start writing their submission even if the work is not finished. Partial work submitted on time beats perfect work submitted never.

### 0:45 to 0:55 Debrief

Ask for three volunteers. If nobody volunteers within eight seconds, call on someone you know is comfortable. Do not let the silence stretch.

Ask each volunteer two questions only:

1. What did you ask?
2. What did you have to change to make it useful?

Do not ask "did it work." That invites a yes or no and teaches nothing.

Then run the one thing that matters most in Week 1. Ask the room: "Did anybody get an answer that looked completely reasonable and turned out to be wrong or unsupported?" Somebody will have one. Thank that person publicly and spend three minutes on it. Wrong answers found by participants are worth more than ten correct answers demonstrated by you.

Close the debrief by naming the pattern out loud: ask, shape, verify.

### 0:55 to 1:00 Submission and close

Restate the submission requirement, restate the deadline, and preview Week 2 in one sentence: "Next week we stop reading and start counting. Bring your patience for spreadsheets."

Post the submission thread link in chat while people are still on the call. Do not wait until after.

## Talking points you can lift directly

On why this matters for the contact center specifically:

> "Average handle time goes up when you are hunting. It does not go up much when you are typing. Every second you spend searching four tabs is a second the member is listening to you breathe."

On accuracy:

> "Copilot is a fast junior analyst with an excellent memory and no judgment. You would not send a junior analyst's first draft to a member without reading it. Same rule."

On the fraud angle, which is worth naming since several people on this team touch disputes:

> "In dispute work the answer and the reason for the answer are two different deliverables. Copilot is good at the first and only good at the second if you make it show its sources. Make it show its sources."

On the ground rule, if someone pushes:

> "The rule is not about trust. It is about the fact that once information leaves a controlled system, you cannot pull it back. We use fake data because fake data cannot hurt anybody."

## Anticipated questions and how to answer them

**"Does this replace searching SharePoint?"**
No. It replaces the part where you did not know which of eleven documents to open. You still open the document.

**"What if it makes something up?"**
It will, occasionally, and usually in a confident tone. That is exactly why the exercise asks you to check every claim against the source pack. Assume anything without a citation is unverified.

**"Can I use this for an actual dispute case?"**
Use it for drafting and for finding policy language. Do not paste member identifying information into it during this boot camp, and follow whatever your compliance guidance says for live work outside this room. If you are unsure, ask before you paste, not after.

**"Why are we using fake data if the real thing is right there?"**
Because we are practicing. Practice with fake data, work with real data under real controls. Mixing the two is how people develop bad habits fast.

**"This is slower than just asking my supervisor."**
Today, maybe. Your supervisor is not available at 4:55 on a Friday. Also, your supervisor answering the same question forty times a month is forty answers they are not giving to something harder.

**"Is this going to be used to measure us?"**
Give a straight answer based on what is actually true in your shop. If adoption is being tracked, say so. If it is not, say so. Hedging here costs you credibility for five more weeks. [ASSUMPTION: you have confirmed the answer before the session]

## Failure modes for you, the facilitator

- **You demo something that works perfectly on the first try.** Participants conclude they are bad at this when their first try is mediocre. Show the mediocre version first, deliberately.
- **You spend fifteen minutes on the data rule.** People stop listening. Say it once with weight, then trust them.
- **You answer a question you do not know the answer to.** Say "I do not know, I will find out by Friday," and then find out by Friday. This is the single fastest way to build credibility with a skeptical room.
- **You let the debrief become a feature tour.** Somebody will want to show an unrelated Copilot feature they discovered. Thank them, park it in a "later" list, and move on. Post the parking lot in the channel after.

## What to capture for your own records

After the session, write down three things in the shared evidence tracker: how many people attended, how many submitted, and one direct quote from a participant about what changed for them. You will want those quotes later and you will not remember them in October.
