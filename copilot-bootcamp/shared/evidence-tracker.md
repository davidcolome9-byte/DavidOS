# Evidence Tracker

For the facilitator. This is how six weeks of sessions becomes something you can cite.

## Why this file exists

Six months from now you will remember that the boot camp went well. You will not remember that eleven people submitted evidence cards, that four of them documented time savings between 30 and 60 percent on a recurring task, or that two participants caught arithmetic errors in AI output during Week 2 and could describe exactly how.

Those specifics are what make a claim credible. Vague claims about a successful program get discounted immediately by anyone who has run one.

Fill this in weekly, within 48 hours of each session, while it is accurate. Reconstructing it later produces numbers that drift in a flattering direction, and people can tell.

## How to use the tables

The tables are short labels only. The detail lives in the bullets underneath each one. Do not put sentences in table cells, because they become unreadable and you will stop maintaining the file.

## Week to outcome map

| Week | Outcome type | Measure | Artifact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verification habit | Submissions with sources | Briefings |
| 2 | Error detection | Errors caught | Verified figures |
| 3 | Peer coaching | Prompts rewritten | Library entries |
| 4 | Risk control | Assertions caught | Three doc sets |
| 5 | Reusable build | Specs completed | Agent specs |
| 6 | Documented gain | Cards submitted | Evidence cards |

### Week 1: verification habit

- **What to count:** attendance, submissions, submissions that cite source documents, submissions that surfaced a conflict or a gap in the source pack.
- **What to capture:** one direct quote from a participant about what changed for them. Get it in writing so you can use it later with permission.
- **The strong outcome:** any participant who identified an unsupported claim in their own output.
- **How to phrase it later:** "Participants produced source referenced briefings from a set of conflicting policy documents and were required to identify what the sources did not answer."
- **What makes this credible:** the "not stated" sections. They demonstrate the training taught people to find gaps, not just to generate text.

### Week 2: error detection

- **What to count:** submissions, submissions containing a verified figure with a stated method, instances where a participant caught Copilot producing a wrong number.
- **What to capture:** the specific arithmetic errors caught, with the participant's name and how they noticed.
- **The strong outcome:** a participant who identified that the data contained multiple independent causes rather than one.
- **How to phrase it later:** "Participants independently identified calculation errors in AI generated analysis during structured verification exercises."
- **What makes this credible:** the specific errors. A claim about verification with no examples reads as a claim about intent.

### Week 3: peer coaching

- **What to count:** submissions, prompts rewritten, prompts contributed to the shared library, instances of participants coaching each other unprompted during breakouts.
- **What to capture:** which of the four elements was missing most often across the cohort. This is a genuine finding about how your team communicates and it applies beyond Copilot.
- **The strong outcome:** peer coaching without a facilitator present, with both names recorded.
- **How to phrase it later:** "Participants diagnosed and repaired failed prompts using a four part framework, then validated their rewrites by having a colleague run them without context."
- **What makes this credible:** the partner test. It shows the program produced transferable artifacts rather than personal tricks.

### Week 4: risk control

- **What to count:** submissions, submissions containing all three documents, unsupported assertions participants caught in their own drafts.
- **What to capture:** the specific claims that got caught, quoted. This is the highest value evidence in the entire program.
- **The strong outcome:** a participant who caught themselves writing a determination the case file did not support and can articulate the difference.
- **How to phrase it later:** "Participants identified and corrected unsupported assertions in AI generated member communications, including instances where a draft stated a finding the case file did not establish."
- **What makes this credible:** this is a control, not adoption. It is the strongest thing you can show anyone who is worried about AI in a regulated function, and it is the strongest thing you can show anyone evaluating whether you understand risk.

### Week 5: reusable build

- **What to count:** completed five part specs, agents actually built, break tests run, failures found and fixed, agents with a named owner and review date.
- **What to capture:** two or three complete specs, in full.
- **The strong outcome:** an agent built by one participant that another participant used.
- **How to phrase it later:** "Participants produced specifications for scoped, source limited agents with explicit guardrails, and validated them through adversarial testing rather than confirmation testing."
- **What makes this credible:** the governance elements. Named owner and review date show you thought about the second year, not just the launch.

### Week 6: documented gain

- **What to count:** presentations delivered, evidence cards submitted, reusable artifacts produced, named two week commitments.
- **What to capture:** every card, consolidated. Plus the "what did not stick" list from the pattern round.
- **The strong outcome:** documented before and after on a recurring workflow, with a stated measurement method.
- **How to phrase it later:** "Participants documented before and after measures on recurring workflows, including methodology, and produced reusable artifacts for colleagues."
- **What makes this credible:** the failures. A program summary with no failures reads as a sales document and gets discounted by exactly the people you want to persuade.

## Quality evidence log

Fill one row per instance. These matter more than the counts.

| Week | Participant | What they caught |
|---|---|---|
| [n] | [name] | [short label] |

Underneath each entry, write two or three sentences of detail. Example format:

- **Week 2, [name], wrong percent change.** Copilot reported a 435 percent increase in new account claims. Participant recalculated by hand and got 336 percent, identified that the model had divided the new value by the old value rather than dividing the difference. Caught before the figure went into a summary.

That level of detail is what turns a count into evidence.

## Promotion packet lines

Draft these as you go, not at the end. Each one needs a number behind it and each number needs a source.

**Program delivery**

> Designed and facilitated a six week Microsoft 365 Copilot boot camp for [n] staff across the digital department and contact center, covering research, analysis, prompt design, written communication, and agent building. [n] participants completed all six sessions.

**Risk and control**

> Built verification and source checking into every module. Participants identified [n] instances of unsupported assertions or calculation errors in AI generated output during structured review exercises, including [specific example].

**Reusable output**

> Produced a shared prompt library of [n] tested prompts and [n] agent specifications with named owners and review dates, covering contact center operations, fraud claim review, quality assurance, reporting, and internal communications.

**Measured outcomes**

> Participants documented before and after measures on recurring workflows. [n] evidence cards recorded time reductions on repeating tasks, with methodology stated. Documented savings ranged from [x] to [y] per week per participant on the workflows measured.

**Peer effect**

> [n] participants committed to and completed a direct handoff of one technique to a named colleague within two weeks of the program ending. [n] agents or prompts built by one participant were adopted by at least one other.

**Honest limits**

> [n] techniques introduced during the program were not in use at the 30 day check in. The most common reasons were [reason] and [reason]. This informed [what you changed].

That last one is not optional. Include it. A packet that reports only wins invites the question of what got left out, and answering that question yourself is much better than having someone else ask it.

## Writing this up later

Write it the way Week 4 taught people to write findings.

State what is established. Do not assert more than the evidence supports. If you have eleven evidence cards showing time savings, you have eleven documented cards. You do not have a department wide productivity improvement, and claiming one will be the first thing a careful reader challenges.

The words to avoid in your own summary are the same ones you taught participants to check: determined, confirmed, proved, established, transformed. Each one is a claim about what you know.

You spent six weeks telling a room full of people that confidence language is a factual claim. Some of them will read what you write about the program.

## Collection checklist

Within 48 hours of each session:

- [ ] Attendance recorded
- [ ] Submissions counted
- [ ] Quality evidence log updated with any catches
- [ ] One quote captured, with permission
- [ ] Artifacts saved somewhere durable

After Week 6:

- [ ] All evidence cards consolidated
- [ ] "What did not stick" list recorded verbatim
- [ ] Running tally finalized
- [ ] Promotion packet lines drafted with real numbers
- [ ] 30 day and 90 day check ins on the calendar

At 30 days:

- [ ] What is still in use, by name
- [ ] What died, and the stated reason
- [ ] Library contributions since the program ended
- [ ] Agents still owned and reviewed

At 90 days:

- [ ] Same four items
- [ ] Anything that spread to someone who never attended

That last line at 90 days is the strongest single piece of evidence the program can produce. A technique reaching somebody who never attended a session means the thing propagated on its own, which is the outcome everybody claims and almost nobody measures.
