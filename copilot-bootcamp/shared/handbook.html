What this is

Read first

Every session is an hour: a short teach, a live demo, twenty minutes where participants do the work themselves, and a debrief. Nobody watches a tool tour. Each week ends with a small submission, and the six weeks end with each person able to describe one improved workflow in sixty seconds, with a number behind it.

One fabricated institution runs through all six weeks, so the work compounds. A problem discovered in the Week 2 data, reopened fraud claims climbing after every other metric recovers, is the same problem the Week 5 agent is built to solve. The Week 4 case file uses the Week 1 policy timelines. None of it is real, and the material says so at the point of use, not just once at the start.

What do I actually have to show for six weeks?

Quick reference

Why a prompt failed

Week 3 · one element, not four

  1. Goal. The outcome, not the topic.
  2. Context. Who reads it and what constrains it.
  3. Source. What it may use.
  4. Shape. Format, length, structure.

Add the missing one and rerun. Rewriting from scratch teaches you nothing about what mattered.

Before you send it

Week 4 · confidence is a claim

Search your own draft for these. Each one asserts something about what you know.

determinedconfirmedestablishedverifiedprovedfound that

Then check the source for every instance. The accurate sentence is usually the clumsier one. Write the clumsier one.

Testing an agent

Week 5 · three tests, in order

  1. In scope. It works. Move on fast.
  2. Next door to its job. Does the guardrail hold, or does it help?
  3. Outside its sources. Does it say so, or fill the gap?

If all three passed, you asked the same question three ways.

Making it stick

Week 6 · triggers, not intentions

An intention decays in about nine days. A trigger attached to something already in your week does not.

I will use Copilot more.

Every Friday, when I build the update, I paste the numbers into this prompt.

Data and accuracy rules

Non negotiable

Stated in Week 1, restated at the point of use

  • Every exercise uses fabricated material for a fictional institution. No real member names, account or card numbers, case identifiers, or screenshots of live systems, in any session.
  • The synthetic policies differ from real regulation on purpose. No timeline, provisional credit rule, or member notice requirement leaves these exercises and enters live work.
  • Five prompts in the library are marked for live case work. Those are de-identified before use or they are not used.
  • Anything acted on gets its source opened and read by a person.
  • Every word that leaves the building has a human owner. Not the tool.

The rule is stated once with weight rather than every five minutes, because constant repetition makes people anxious instead of careful. It is then restated at the point of use in the three sessions that get closest to live work: before anyone drafts a member letter, before anyone builds anything, and before anyone presents a real workflow.

What the program produces

Evidence model

Each week is designed to leave a specific artifact behind. The facilitator records these within forty eight hours of each session, while they are still accurate, and participants are told in Week 1 that the record exists and what it is for. Names go in it only with spoken permission.

Each week maps to one outcome type and one thing you can point at later.

The strongest outcomes are the caught errors, not the adoption counts. A participant who finds a wrong figure in generated analysis, or an unsupported claim in their own draft, demonstrates that the training produced a control rather than enthusiasm. The program summary reports what did not stick alongside what did, because a write up with no failures gets discounted by exactly the people worth persuading.
