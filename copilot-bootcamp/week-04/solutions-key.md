# Week 4 Solutions Key

Facilitator copy. Do not distribute before the read aloud.

## What the case file actually establishes

You need this clear in your head before anyone reads their letter out loud.

**Established:**
- Four transactions were disputed, totaling 412.60
- Three of them came from the member's known device in the member's home city
- The member had two prior undisputed purchases at that same merchant
- One transaction came from an unknown device in a different state and was credited
- Provisional credit was issued in full on business day 10
- 261.60 was reversed on closing, 151.00 was retained by the member
- The member has not been notified

**Not established, and this is the important list:**
- That the member authorized T1, T2, or T3. The file says insufficient evidence to support unauthorized use, which is a statement about the evidence, not about the member.
- That the member filed a false claim
- Whether any appeal or reconsideration path exists, or what the member may request from us
- When the reversal posted
- Whether the account went negative
- Whether anyone tried to reach the member before reversing

**The four things a strong submission catches:**

1. The claim was partial. T4 was credited. A letter that reads as a full denial is factually wrong.
2. "Insufficient evidence" is not "authorized." Any drift between those is the central error of the week.
3. The date inconsistency in the member's statement belongs in the case note only. In a member letter it functions as an accusation.
4. Reversing without advance notice, with no reversal date recorded, is a supervisor level item. It goes at the top of the summary, not in a closing paragraph.

If a participant catches three of those four, they had a strong session.

## Worked example of a strong response

### Supervisor summary, 87 words

> **SYN-2025-04417, Nolan, closed day 14. Two items need you.**
>
> Outcome: partial. T4 credited, 151.00 retained. T1 to T3 closed as insufficient evidence, 261.60 reversed.
>
> **Needs your call.** The member was not told in advance that the reversal was coming, and the file does not record the reversal date. If it posted before notification, we may have a member with an unexplained debit and no letter. Outcome letter is due within 3 business days and has not gone out.
>
> Everything else routine. No prior claims on this account.

**Why this works.** It opens with what needs a decision, not with background. It states the partial outcome correctly in one line. The problem is named plainly without dressing it up. Under 100 words and readable on a phone.

### Case note

> **CLAIM SYN-2025-04417. Closing note.**
>
> **Observed.**
> Four card not present transactions disputed, total 412.60. T1 to T3 at SYNTH-MART, T4 at SYNTH-GOODS.
> T1 to T3 device fingerprint matched the account's established online banking device, in use 14 months. Geolocation matched member home city.
> Two prior completed, undisputed purchases at SYNTH-MART in the previous six months.
> T4 device fingerprint not previously seen on this account. Geolocation out of state.
> Card never reported lost or stolen. No reported credential compromise. No prior claims on this account.
> Member stated at intake the transactions occurred on the 14th. On day 6 the member stated the 12th and said they may have been confused about the date.
> Member declined a police report and was advised one was not required.
>
> **Concluded.**
> T1 to T3: insufficient evidence to support unauthorized use. Device, location, and merchant history are all consistent with the account's established pattern. This is a finding about the evidence available. It is not a finding that the member authorized the transactions and it should not be recorded or communicated as one.
> T4: supported. Unknown device and out of state geolocation are inconsistent with the account pattern.
>
> **Actions taken.**
> Provisional credit 412.60 issued day 10. On closing, 261.60 reversed, 151.00 retained.
>
> **Open.**
> Member not yet notified. Written notice due within 3 business days of day 14.
> Reversal date not recorded in this file. Needs to be established.
> No advance notice of reversal was given to the member.
> No appeal or reconsideration path is documented in this file. If one exists it is not recorded here.

**Why this works.** Observed and Concluded are separate sections, so a colleague six months out can tell what was seen from what was inferred. The conclusion explicitly states what the finding is not, which prevents the next person from escalating it. Open items are listed rather than narrated.

### Member letter

> Dear Casey Nolan,
>
> We have finished reviewing the four transactions you reported on your Riverstone debit card.
>
> **What we found**
>
> For one transaction, 151.00 at SYNTH-GOODS, our review supported your claim. That credit stays in your account. You do not need to do anything.
>
> For the other three transactions, totaling 261.60, we were not able to find enough evidence to support that they were unauthorized. Because of this, the temporary credit we issued for those three transactions has been reversed.
>
> **What this means for your account**
>
> You keep the 151.00 credit. We removed 261.60 from your account on [date]. For five business days from the date of this letter, we will pay checks, automatic payments, and other items we would have paid if that credit were still there, and we will not charge you an overdraft fee as a result of removing it.
>
> **Documents we used**
>
> You have the right to ask us for copies of the documents we relied on to reach this decision. Call the number below or write to us and we will send them to you promptly, at no charge.
>
> **If you disagree**
>
> If you have information we did not have, such as a receipt, a statement from the merchant, or details about where you were, please contact us. We will review anything new you can provide.
>
> If you have questions about this letter, call us at the number on the back of your card.
>
> Riverstone Credit Union

**Why this works.** It states the partial outcome in the first line of findings, so the member does not read three paragraphs believing they lost everything. It uses "we were not able to find enough evidence" rather than any verb asserting the member authorized anything. It says nothing about the date inconsistency. It runs to about 230 words, which is why the exercise allows 250: the required disclosures cost roughly 80 words and they are not the place to economize.

**Their unsupported assertion catch:**

> "My first draft said 'our investigation determined that these transactions were authorized by you.' The case file never says that. It says insufficient evidence. I changed it to 'we were not able to find enough evidence to support that they were unauthorized.' It is a clumsier sentence and it is the accurate one."

**Their omission note:**

> "I left out that the member changed the date from the 14th to the 12th. It belongs in the case note because the next investigator needs it. In a letter to the member it reads as us calling them a liar, and it does not change what we decided."

## Say this in the debrief before anyone leaves

The letter above is deliberately incomplete as a real dispute denial notice, because the synthetic case file does not contain the pieces a real one needs. In a live shop, a letter that debits a provisional credit has to state the date and amount of the debit and has to tell the member that items will be honored without an overdraft charge for five business days. A letter finding no error has to state the member's right to request the documents relied on. Neither requirement is anywhere in this exercise's source material.

Say that out loud. Do not let anyone leave believing this letter is a template. The exercise teaches how to avoid asserting what you cannot support. It does not teach notice content, and the gap between those two things is where people get hurt.

## Failure mode one: insufficient evidence becomes a verdict

**What it looks like.** The member letter contains a sentence like:

> "Our investigation determined that these transactions were authorized by you and were not fraudulent."

Or the softer, equally wrong version:

> "We confirmed that the transactions were made using your device and were valid purchases."

Both read as competent professional writing. Both accuse the member of filing a false claim, and the case file does not support that.

**Why it happens.** Two reasons working together.

The model prefers clean sentences. "We were unable to substantiate that the transactions were unauthorized" is a double negative wrapped in hedging. "We determined the transactions were authorized" is crisp. Fluency optimization pushes toward the crisp version every time.

And the participant reads it approvingly, because it sounds like a finished letter. Nobody proofreads for accusations they did not intend to make.

**What it costs.** In a real shop, this is not a tone note. A letter telling a member you determined they authorized transactions, when your actual finding was that the evidence was insufficient, misstates the outcome of an investigation in a written communication to a member. That is a different category of problem than awkward phrasing, and it is worth saying that plainly in the room.

**The tell.** Search the submission for: determined, confirmed, established, verified, found that, valid, legitimate. Then check the file.

**How to fix it in the room.** Put the two sentences on screen side by side and ask the room which one the case file supports. Everyone will get it right in three seconds when the sentences are next to each other. That is the whole lesson: the error is invisible in flowing text and obvious in comparison. Tell them to build the comparison habit, since they will not have you standing next to them.

**What to say:** "Nobody in this room would say out loud that we proved the member lied. Several of us just wrote it. That is what fluent output does. It writes the confident version and you read past it because it sounds finished."

## Failure mode two: three documents that are one document

**What it looks like.** The participant produces a supervisor summary, a case note, and a member letter that contain the same content in the same order at three different lengths. The member letter includes device fingerprint analysis. The supervisor summary opens with intake background instead of the notification problem. The case note is a narrative paragraph with no separation between what was seen and what was concluded.

**Why it happens.** The participant asked for "a summary, a case note, and a letter" in one prompt without describing three different readers. The model has one set of facts and no reason to reorder them, so it compresses and expands the same structure. It looks like three documents because they are three different lengths.

**Why it matters more than it looks.** Two separate consequences.

Putting internal reasoning in a member letter is a genuine problem. A member does not need to know about device fingerprint matching, and telling them creates questions you do not want to answer in writing.

Burying the notification issue in the supervisor summary means the supervisor does not act on it. The one thing that actually needed a decision this week gets read as background.

**The tell.** All three documents open with the same information. Read only the first sentence of each. If they match, the participant wrote one document three times.

**How to fix it in the room.** Have them state, out loud, what question each reader is trying to answer. Supervisor: do I need to do anything. Case note: can I pick this up cold. Member: what happened to my money. Then ask whether the first sentence of each document answers that reader's question. Usually two of three fail and they can see it instantly.

**What to say:** "Length is not audience. If the only difference between your three documents is word count, you wrote one document and cut it twice."

## Grading guidance

**On track.** Partial outcome stated correctly in all three. No verb asserting authorization. Case note separates observed from concluded. Supervisor summary leads with the notification problem. Named a real omission with a real reason.

**Needs a nudge.** Documents are good but the member letter reads as a full denial, or the notification gap is buried. Reply with one line: "Read only the first sentence of each of your three documents. Do they answer three different questions?"

**Needs a conversation.** Any submission asserting the member authorized the transactions, especially if the participant did not catch it in their own audit. Handle this directly and privately, and handle it kindly, because the person is not careless, they are experiencing exactly the effect the session was built to demonstrate. Ask them to redo just the letter. It takes them five minutes and it is the most durable thing they will take out of this boot camp.

## What to log for the evidence tracker

Every caught unsupported assertion, with the specific claim. These are the strongest outcomes in the program because they show the training produced a control, not just usage. "Participants identified and corrected unsupported assertions in AI generated member communications during structured review" is a defensible sentence only if you wrote down the instances.

Also grab two or three sanitized three document sets. Same facts written three ways is an unusually clear artifact when you need to show somebody what this program actually taught.
