# Week 4 Knowledge Check: Writing Coach

Five questions. Answer from memory first, then check yourself. Takes about five minutes.

---

**Question 1**

A case file states "insufficient evidence to support unauthorized use." Which sentence can you write in a member letter?

A. We determined the transactions were authorized by you.
B. We confirmed these were valid purchases you made.
C. We were not able to find enough evidence to support that these were unauthorized.
D. Our investigation found no fraud occurred.

---

**Question 2**

You produce a supervisor summary, a case note, and a member letter, and all three open with the same sentence. What does that tell you?

A. You were consistent, which is good
B. You wrote one document three times at three lengths
C. The case file was too short
D. Nothing, opening lines do not matter much

---

**Question 3**

The member changed their account of the transaction date between intake and a follow up call. Where does that belong?

A. In all three documents, since it is a fact
B. In the member letter, so they know we noticed
C. In the case note only
D. Nowhere, since it did not change the outcome

---

**Question 4**

Which use of Copilot does the most to protect the accuracy of a document you are sending?

A. Asking it to generate the document from scratch
B. Asking it to make the document sound more professional
C. Asking it to list every claim in your draft that the source does not support
D. Asking it whether the document is accurate

---

**Question 5**

A fraud claim was partially supported: one transaction credited, three reversed. Your member letter describes the outcome as a denial. What kind of error is this?

A. A tone problem
B. A length problem
C. A factual error about the outcome of the investigation
D. Not an error, since most of the claim was denied

---
---

## Answer key

**1. C.** We were not able to find enough evidence.

A, B, and D all assert findings the file does not contain. "Insufficient evidence" is a statement about what the investigation could establish. "Authorized by you" is a statement about what the member did, and it functions as an accusation that they filed a false claim. These are different findings and the gap between them is the central idea of this week. The accurate sentence is clumsier. Write the clumsier sentence.

**2. B.** You wrote one document three times.

Three readers ask three different questions. A supervisor asks whether she needs to do anything. A case note answers whether a colleague can pick it up cold. A member asks what happened to their money. If all three open the same way, at most one of them opens correctly. Length is not audience.

**3. C.** The case note only.

The next investigator needs it, because an inconsistent account is relevant context for anyone reviewing the file later. In a letter to the member it does not change the outcome and it reads as calling them a liar. Deciding what to leave out is the actual skill this week teaches, and this is the clearest example of it.

**4. C.** Asking it to list unsupported claims.

This is the editing move. It uses the model for consistency checking, which it is genuinely good at, while leaving your judgment in the document. Option D is the trap and it is the same trap as Week 1: asking a tool to grade its own output is not a control. Option B changes register without changing accuracy, and "more professional" often means "more confident," which makes the problem worse.

**5. C.** A factual error about the outcome.

The member keeps 151.00. A letter that reads as a full denial misstates what happened to their money, which is the one thing the letter exists to communicate. A member who reads it, believes they lost everything, and then sees a partial credit on their statement will call, and the call will not go well. First drafts make this error often, because the file reads mostly like a denial and the exception is one line.

---

## If you missed two or more

Focus on question 1. Write the two sentences out side by side and read them aloud. If you can feel the difference between a finding about evidence and a finding about a person, you have the idea, and everything else this week follows from it.
