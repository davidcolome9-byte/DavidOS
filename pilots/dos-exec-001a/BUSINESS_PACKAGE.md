# Starwhisk Bakehouse — Synthetic Business Package

> **Synthetic-use notice:** Starwhisk Bakehouse is a fictional business
> created only for DOS-EXEC-001A. All evidence, metrics, products, names,
> quotes, prices, schedules, contact details, and commercial assumptions
> below are invented. No web research, real-business data, customer data,
> or outreach was used.

## Fictional bakery profile

**Identity:** Starwhisk Bakehouse — fictional neighborhood bakery
concept

**Fictional location:** Exampletown, ZZ

**Fictional stage:** Four-year-old retail bakery with one storefront and
an inconsistent preorder habit

**Fictional operating model:** Three public bake days per week, a narrow
core menu, seasonal specials, small celebration cakes, and occasional
office boxes

**Fictional customer promise:** Familiar baked goods with a small,
memorable sense of occasion

**Rough fictional opportunity:** The bakehouse earns attention through
its products but makes customers work too hard to understand what is
available, when to order, and which option fits a weekday pickup,
weekend table, gift, or small celebration.

### Synthetic audience segments

1. **Morning regular:** wants today’s hours and reliable staple
   availability in under a minute.
2. **Weekend host:** wants a shareable box or loaf without assembling an
   order item by item.
3. **Small celebration buyer:** wants a clear cake starting point,
   serving guidance, lead time, and next step.
4. **Team organizer:** wants an office-friendly box with transparent
   quantity and pickup guidance.
5. **Gift buyer:** wants something that feels specific to the bakery and
   is easy to give.

## Opportunity analysis

### Core opportunity

Make Starwhisk easier to choose, not merely nicer to look at. The
strongest fictional opportunity is a short path from:

**“That looks good” → “I understand the offer” → “I know when and how to
pick it up.”**

The current synthetic weaknesses imply three practical levers:

1. **Clarity:** stable anchors, visible timing, explicit use cases.
2. **Packaging:** named boxes and simple celebration starting points
   increase decision confidence.
3. **Memory:** one coherent visual/verbal system makes the bakery easier
   to recall and recommend.

## Recommended service offer

### Offer name

**Preorder & Celebration Starter — fictional service concept**

### Intended outcome

Give one small bakery a clear, branded local website starting point that
helps a visitor understand signature products, plan a pickup, and
recognize the celebration path without requiring a new commerce
platform.

### Scope

1. One 60-minute discovery session using only business-approved facts.
2. One concise opportunity and messaging brief.
3. One five-section responsive static website.
4. Up to six real products after factual verification.
5. One preorder-path prototype stopping before payment or fulfillment.
6. One accessibility and mobile validation pass.
7. One handoff guide for a future hosting or commerce decision.

### Explicit exclusions

- no ordering, inventory, payment, account, loyalty, email, SMS, CRM,
  analytics, or delivery integration;
- no brand-name availability, trademark, legal, nutrition, allergen, or
  regulatory advice;
- no photography production or social campaign;
- no publishing or domain work without a separate authorization;
- no unsupported claims, fabricated reviews, or invented operating facts.

### Synthetic commercial assumption

**Illustrative fixed fee: $3,800**, with 50% at kickoff and 50% at
approved local handoff. This is an invented planning figure, not a quote,
market benchmark, or recommendation for a real transaction.

### Fictional acceptance criteria

- a first-time visitor can name the three signature offers;
- hours, pickup rhythm, and preorder boundary are visible without a
  policy hunt;
- the local prototype has no broken link or missing asset;
- mobile layout works at 375 CSS pixels without horizontal overflow;
- keyboard users can reach and operate navigation, estimator, and FAQ;
- every business fact is verified before any real adaptation;
- the package stops before hosting, payment, contact, or publication.

## Do-nothing alternative

Choosing no action is a valid outcome for the fictional bakery,
especially if production capacity, cash, or staff attention is more
constrained than the digital decision path.

| Decision factor | If the bakery takes no action |
|---|---|
| What remains unchanged | The fictional menu stays rotating, pickup and cutoff details remain distributed across current materials, celebration items remain less visible, and staff continue answering availability questions manually. |
| Risks that remain | Customers may still miss cake lead times or signature products, office buyers may lack serving guidance, and staff may continue repeating basic pickup information. These are synthetic risks, not measured losses. |
| Costs avoided | The bakery avoids the illustrative `$3,800` project fee, content-preparation time, photography or legal-review costs, staff review time, hosting decisions, and maintenance obligations. |
| Low-effort manual alternatives | Pin one accurate weekly menu on the existing channel, keep a printed pickup/cutoff card by the phone, use a paper cake-inquiry checklist, and record recurring counter questions with tally marks. |
| Why no action may be reasonable | If order volume is manageable, facts change too often, margins or capacity are uncertain, or the owner cannot maintain accurate content, keeping a familiar manual process may be safer than adding a website. |

No-action costs and risks should be revisited only with real,
client-supplied evidence. Inaction is not treated as irrational or as
permission to pressure the owner.

## Assumptions and limitations

### Synthetic assumptions

- The fictional bakery wants a warm, slightly whimsical identity.
- Three anchor products can remain stable long enough to aid recognition.
- Pickup, not delivery, is the initial fulfillment model.
- A real operator could provide verified product, allergen, timing,
  capacity, and price data during discovery.
- A static prototype is valuable before selecting commerce tooling.

### Known limitations

- No real prospect, customer, market, competitor, traffic, revenue, or
  operational data was analyzed.
- The estimator is deliberately not an order form and proves no
  fulfillment or payment workflow.
- Accessibility checks are basic and representative, not a formal
  conformance audit.
- Responsive checks cover representative viewports, not every browser,
  device, zoom level, language length, or assistive technology.
- The fictional name has not been searched or cleared for real use.
- Product economics omit major costs and cannot support a real pricing
  decision.
- Real adaptation requires new authorization, factual discovery, claim
  review, legal/compliance input where applicable, and separate hosting
  and integration choices.
